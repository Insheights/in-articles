# LinkedIn post: Product Mindset Is How the Whole Company Should Think

The companion post for [this article](article.md), as published.

---

Most companies treat product mindset as a product manager's job.

Then finance funds fixed-scope projects. Sales promises features to close deals. Operations counts tickets closed. Everyone does their job well, and nobody practices a product mindset.

One person can't practice it for everyone else.

Product mindset works when it's how the whole company thinks, guided by five principles:\
→ Customer obsession: start from someone's outcome, not your own process\
→ Outcome over output: what changed matters more than what you produced\
→ Continuous discovery: learning never stops at kickoff\
→ Hypothesis-driven thinking: make every bet explicit, including what would prove it wrong\
→ Cross-functional ownership: one team owns the outcome end to end

Here's what it changes, using an illustrative company selling inventory software to distributors.

STRATEGY\
Before: "Launch a mobile app, add forecasting, expand partners."\
After: "Grow distributor revenue to $40M by 2028, winning because we connect to the warehouse systems they already run."

TRANSFORMATION\
Before: "Reorganize customer operations and roll out a new CRM."\
After: "Move onboarding to one customer team, cutting time to live data from 90 days to 30."

VALUE DELIVERY\
Before: "Ship two features and bring 40 distributors live."\
After: "Distributors reduce stockouts within 60 days of going live, shown by fewer emergency orders."

Each "before" counts what gets produced. Each "after" names what changes, and for whom.

And they connect: revenue only comes if distributors renew; they renew only if stockouts fall; and that can't happen while onboarding takes three months.

A simple test for your next leadership meeting:\
1. What customer outcome is our biggest initiative meant to change, and how will we know?\
2. What would tell us in 90 days that it's the wrong bet?\
3. Which single team owns that outcome end to end?

If the answers depend on whom you ask, product mindset lives in a role, not in the organization.
