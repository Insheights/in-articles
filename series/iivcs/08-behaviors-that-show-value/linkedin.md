# LinkedIn post: Adoption, Engagement, Retention: The Behaviors That Show Value Before Results Do

The companion post for [this article](article.md), as published.

---

Two dashboards said the same thing: it worked.

Six weeks after two launches at Stocklight, the illustrative company in my value creation series:\
→ For customers, reorder from an alert: 1,140 uses in the first week, and 4 of 4 pilot distributors "adopted".\
→ For Stocklight's own implementation specialists, an AI assistant: 92% active users, and 4.5 hours a week saved, self-reported.

Neither said anything about value.\
→ At one "adopted" distributor, a single manager placed most of the orders while six colleagues kept re-keying them.\
→ The specialists spent the assistant on email and status notes. The work that set time to first value still happened by hand, in spreadsheets.

Value can't be observed directly. The behavior of the people it's for can:
1. Adoption: a first real use, on the work it was built for. Not a login, a license, or a click.
2. Engagement: they rely on it, at the depth the value needs, instead of the old way.
3. Retention: they keep choosing it after the launch attention fades.

Adoption and Engagement are leading indicators. The team steers by them weekly. Retention and the outcomes behind it are lagging: they confirm, later, whether the team steered well. A leading indicator earns its place only if it predicts a lagging one.

Rebuilt around those behaviors, the indicators started earning their keep. Engagement showed within two weeks that managers didn't trust the suggested order quantities, long before any renewal could have. Once the assistant was connected to the configuration work, configuration time per distributor fell from 22 hours to 9, and time to first value moved from 52 days to 43.

For internal tools, one lesson matters most: productivity that stops at the employee isn't value yet. Name the customer outcome before the rollout.

The article includes a template for an AER (Adoption, Engagement, Retention) indicator set: what you steer by, what confirms it, and how often each is read.
