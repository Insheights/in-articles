# LinkedIn post: Where Value Flow Leaks in Your Operating Model

The companion post for [this article](article.md), as published.

---

On paper, strategies never fail. Execution does.

At least, that's how it usually gets explained. Admitting a strategy was wrong can feel like admitting your judgment was wrong, and few organizations reward leaders for that.

But more often, nobody failed. The strategy lost value a little at a time, at every point where work changed hands.

In my new article, I trace one strategic intent through a company, backward from the customer to the boardroom, and find four leaks:\
→ Feedback: what customers reveal never reaches the people setting strategy\
→ Adoption, engagement, and retention: customers and employees get access, not value\
→ Fragmentation and handoffs: every team owns a piece, and work waits between them\
→ Translation: intent becomes deliverables that are misaligned or incomplete

Every team hit its targets. The outcome still failed.

People don't cause the leaks. The operating model does, and fear of failure keeps them hidden.

The article includes real cases from Nokia, Quibi, Boeing, and Wells Fargo, four shifts leaders can make, and a simple exercise to find the leaks in your own organization.
