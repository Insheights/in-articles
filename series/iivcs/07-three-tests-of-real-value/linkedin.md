# LinkedIn post: Efficiency, Experience, Effectiveness: The Three Tests of Real Value

The companion post for [this article](article.md), as published.

---

Every outcome passed. Every test failed.

Three months into a pilot at Stocklight, the illustrative company in my framework series, the review looked great: 78% of inventory managers acted on alerts, emergency orders dropped 24%, and onboarding time fell from 90 days to 41.

Then the team visited the customers.\
→ Managers were clearing 30 to 40 alerts a day. That was more than an hour of extra work.\
→ They didn't trust the alerts, so they double-checked everything in a spreadsheet.\
→ Some cut emergency orders by quietly raising safety stock, tying up cash.

The outcomes moved. The value didn't.

An outcome measured one way can move without creating value. The three tests every outcome has to pass, in order:\
1. Efficiency (Use): can stakeholders use what's delivered with less effort, not more?\
2. Experience (Feel): do they feel the difference, and trust it enough to rely on it?\
3. Effectiveness (Achieve): do they get the result they came for?

The order matters. People can't feel value they can't use, and they won't stay long enough to achieve anything with value they don't trust.

The article includes a template for a 3E outcome profile, with the one blank most teams skip: the workaround that would fake your outcome.
